Know the Difference

Botox vs. Dysport

Both are highly effective neuromodulators with strong safety profiles. The key differences lie in their spread patterns and onset speed. Your provider will recommend the best option for your anatomy and goals.

Botox Dysport
Onset 3-7 days 2-5 days
Spread Pattern More precise, stays localized Broader diffusion, covers wider areas
Best For Targeted areas requiring precision (crow's feet, lip flip) Larger areas (forehead, frown lines)
Duration 3-4 months 3-4 months

Botox & Dysport FAQ

How long does Botox last?

Results typically last 3 to 4 months, though this varies by individual metabolism, treatment area, and dosing. With consistent treatments, many patients find their results last longer over time as the targeted muscles gradually weaken from repeated relaxation.

Does Botox hurt?

Most patients describe the sensation as a brief, mild pinch. The needles used are extremely fine, and the injections take only a few seconds per site. No anesthesia is required, though ice or topical numbing can be applied for sensitive areas upon request.

At what age should I start Botox?

There is no single right age. Many patients in their late 20s and early 30s begin preventive treatments to slow the formation of dynamic wrinkles before they become permanent static lines. Others start later when existing lines become more noticeable at rest. Your provider will assess your skin and goals during your consultation.

Can Botox treat TMJ or jaw clenching?

Yes. Botox is highly effective for TMJ dysfunction and bruxism (jaw clenching). Injections into the masseter muscle relax the jaw, reducing pain, tension headaches, and tooth grinding. Many patients also notice a slimming effect along the jawline as the overworked muscle relaxes and reduces in size over time.
