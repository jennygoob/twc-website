The Science

The Controlled Injury Response

Microneedling works by harnessing your body's own repair mechanisms. Each micro-channel initiates a three-phase biological response that produces new, structurally sound collagen and elastin.

I

Penetration

Immediate — Controlled Disruption

Fine needles penetrate 1.5 to 2.5mm into the dermis, creating thousands of micro-channels through the epidermis and into the deeper tissue layers. Each channel is precise enough to trigger a healing response without causing scarring. The controlled depth ensures the injury reaches the collagen-producing fibroblasts while leaving the surrounding tissue structurally intact.

II

Collagen Cascade

Days 1-14 — Inflammatory & Proliferative Response

The body recognizes the micro-injuries and activates its wound-healing response. Platelets aggregate, growth factors are released, and fibroblasts begin producing new collagen and elastin. This proliferative phase floods the treated area with the raw materials of skin regeneration. If PRP is applied during treatment, concentrated growth factors amplify this cascade significantly.

III

Remodeling

Weeks 4-12 — Structural Rebuilding

New collagen matures and reorganizes into stronger, more ordered tissue. Skin texture smooths, scars soften, pores tighten, and overall firmness improves. This remodeling phase continues for months after treatment, which is why results compound with each session and continue to develop long after the last appointment.

Indications

What It Treats

Microneedling addresses concerns that originate in the dermal layer — where topical products and surface treatments cannot reach.

Acne Scars
Breaks down scar tissue and triggers new collagen to fill pitted, rolling, and boxcar scars. Multiple sessions progressively smooth the surface as new tissue replaces damaged architecture.
Fine Lines
Stimulates collagen in the superficial dermis where early aging lines form. Repeated treatments rebuild the structural support that prevents fine lines from becoming deep wrinkles.
Pore Size
New collagen tightens the tissue around enlarged pores, reducing their visible diameter. Results are most noticeable on the nose, cheeks, and chin where pores are most prominent.
Uneven Texture
Smooths rough, bumpy, or uneven skin by promoting uniform cellular turnover and dermal remodeling across the treated area.
Stretch Marks
Penetrates through the atrophied tissue of stretch marks and initiates collagen regeneration within the damaged area. Effective on both newer and older marks.
Hyperpigmentation
Accelerates turnover of pigmented cells and normalizes melanin distribution. Works on sun damage, post-inflammatory marks, and melasma when combined with appropriate topical protocols.
Know the Difference

Medical-Grade vs. At-Home

Depth determines outcome. At-home rollers operate in the superficial epidermis. Clinical microneedling reaches the collagen-producing dermis where structural change actually happens.

Medical-Grade

1.5 — 2.5mm Penetration

Clinical-depth treatment administered by a licensed provider with real-time depth control and sterile, single-use cartridges.

  • Reaches the reticular dermis where fibroblasts produce collagen
  • Adjustable depth per treatment zone (forehead vs cheeks vs jawline)
  • Motorized device ensures consistent, uniform penetration
  • Can be combined with PRP for amplified growth factor delivery
  • Produces measurable structural improvement in 3-6 sessions
  • Treats acne scars, stretch marks, and moderate skin laxity

At-Home Rollers

0.1 — 0.25mm Penetration

Surface-level tools that create superficial disruption without reaching the dermal layer where collagen is produced.

  • Stays within the epidermis — no dermal stimulation
  • Fixed, shallow depth with no adjustment capability
  • Manual rolling creates inconsistent channel depth and spacing
  • May improve product absorption marginally
  • Cannot address scarring, laxity, or structural concerns
  • Risk of infection if needles dull or are reused without proper sterilization
10x
Clinical microneedling penetrates up to ten times deeper than at-home rollers — reaching the collagen-producing dermis where real structural change begins.

Microneedling FAQ

Does microneedling hurt?

Topical numbing cream is applied 20 to 30 minutes before treatment, which significantly reduces sensation. Most patients describe the feeling as a mild vibrating pressure across the skin. Comfort levels are very manageable, and your provider adjusts needle depth and speed throughout the session based on your feedback and the treatment zone.

What is the downtime?

Expect 24 to 72 hours of mild redness, similar to a light sunburn. Some patients experience minor swelling or skin tightness for the first day. Most return to normal activities within 1 to 2 days. Avoid direct sun, active skincare ingredients like retinoids and AHAs, and heavy makeup for 48 to 72 hours post-treatment.

How many sessions do I need?

Most patients see meaningful improvement with 3 to 6 sessions spaced 4 to 6 weeks apart. Fine lines and texture may improve in 3 sessions. Deeper acne scarring or stretch marks typically require 4 to 6 sessions. Your provider will design a protocol based on your skin assessment and goals.

Can I combine microneedling with PRP?

Yes, and it is one of the most effective combinations available. After the micro-channels are created, PRP is applied topically and driven into the skin, delivering concentrated growth factors directly into the treatment zone. This combination accelerates healing and amplifies collagen production beyond what either treatment achieves alone.
