How Infrared Differs

Traditional saunas heat the air around you to extreme temperatures. Infrared saunas use light wavelengths to heat your body directly — penetrating 1.5 to 2 inches into tissue for deeper therapeutic effect at a more comfortable ambient temperature. The result: more efficient detoxification, better pain relief, and a more tolerable experience.

Deeper

Tissue Penetration

Infrared light reaches deep into muscle and connective tissue — heating your body from the inside rather than just the surface. This triggers a more productive sweat response and deeper therapeutic effect.

Comfortable

Lower Temperature

Infrared saunas operate at 120-150 degrees — significantly lower than the 150-195 degrees of traditional saunas. You get the therapeutic benefits without the suffocating heat.

Frequently Asked Questions

How is infrared different from a traditional sauna?

Traditional saunas heat the air around you to 150-195 degrees. Infrared saunas use light wavelengths to heat your body directly at a lower ambient temperature of 120-150 degrees. This means deeper tissue penetration, more efficient detoxification, and a more comfortable experience — you get the therapeutic benefits without the extreme heat.

How long is a session?

Standard sessions are 30 minutes. This is sufficient time for deep-tissue heating, activation of sweat response, and therapeutic benefit. Your provider can recommend session duration and frequency based on your goals.

How often should I use the infrared sauna?

Most patients benefit from 2-3 sessions per week for active health goals such as detoxification, pain management, or athletic recovery. Once-weekly sessions work well for general wellness and relaxation. CLARITY members can integrate sauna therapy into their overall optimization protocol.

Is infrared sauna safe?

Yes. Infrared sauna therapy is well-tolerated by most people. The lower ambient temperature makes it more comfortable than traditional saunas. We recommend staying hydrated before, during, and after your session. Patients with certain medical conditions should consult with their provider before beginning sauna therapy.
