Women's Hormone Health

Menopause Isn't the End of Anything. It's the Beginning of Data.

Hot flashes, insomnia, mood swings, brain fog, weight gain — you have been told this is just part of the transition. It does not have to be. Every symptom maps to a measurable hormonal shift. And measurable means manageable.

The Transition

Three Phases. Different Symptoms. One Solution.

The hormonal shift does not happen overnight. It unfolds across three distinct phases — each with different symptoms, different needs, and different data to guide treatment.

Phase 1

Perimenopause

Typically 38-48

Hormones begin fluctuating unpredictably. You may not have missed a period yet, but your body is already changing.

  • Irregular cycles
  • New-onset anxiety or insomnia
  • Temperature dysregulation
  • Mood volatility
Phase 2

Menopause

Average age 51

Defined as 12 consecutive months without menstruation. Estrogen and progesterone reach their lowest sustained levels.

  • Hot flashes and night sweats
  • Vaginal dryness
  • Cognitive decline
  • Accelerated bone density loss
Phase 3

Post-Menopause

Ongoing

Symptoms may ease but cardiovascular risk rises. Biological aging accelerates without hormonal support.

  • Cardiovascular risk increase
  • Osteoporosis progression
  • Metabolic slowdown
  • Skin and tissue changes
The Biology

What's Actually Changing Inside

Menopause is not one hormone declining. It is the simultaneous shift of estrogen, progesterone, testosterone, and their downstream effects on thyroid, cortisol, and metabolism. The symptoms you feel are the overlap zone of all of them.

That is why treating one hormone in isolation rarely resolves the full picture. We map all of them — so the protocol addresses the system, not a single number.

  • Estradiol

    Primary estrogen. Decline drives hot flashes, bone loss, cardiovascular risk, and vaginal atrophy. We track estradiol — not just total estrogen.

  • Progesterone

    The calming hormone. Drops first during perimenopause, causing anxiety, insomnia, and cycle irregularity before estrogen declines.

  • Testosterone

    Women produce testosterone too — and it declines 50% by menopause. Drives libido, energy, muscle tone, and cognitive sharpness.

  • Thyroid

    Menopausal hormonal shifts alter thyroid binding and conversion. Many women develop subclinical hypothyroidism during the transition without realizing it.

  • Cortisol & DHEA-S

    The stress axis shifts during menopause. Cortisol rises as DHEA-S declines — compounding fatigue, weight gain, and mood disruption.
